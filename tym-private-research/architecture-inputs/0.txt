Spatial-Temporal and Semantic Information Extraction Boghiu Șerban Paul Faculty of Computer Science Alexandru Ioan
Cuza University of Iași serban.boghiu@info.uaic.ro Abstract — This document continues the research of extracting
and processing events from a temporal, spatial and semantical point of view, but in a more experimental and
technical way. Technologies that can be used in an implementation of the Time Yards Model (TYM) are presented and a
brief comparison between them is made. A use case of text interpretation is also presented with results in
analyzing past hazards and predicting potential others, which could be inspiring for a generalization of TYM in
which not only human characters are traced in time, but also political developments, pandemics or other events that
involve large masses of people. Keywords—action, time, temporality, named entity recognition, geoparsing, natural
text processing, hazards I. I NTRODUCTION Spatial-temporal information is present all around us in a large variety
of types of text: literature, daily news, posts, chats, etc. Hence, the need of processing them automatically and
organizing their content up to the level that allows the rather unstructured textual data to be placed in
representation forms that can trigger automated or machine-aided reasoning and other complex tasks that mimic the
way humans interpret texts. The concepts of time and space play a significant role in our life, and many modern
approaches are oriented today towards analyzing and modeling them in a spatial-temporal space (Klimešová, Dana and
Eva Ocelíková, 2012, “Spatial-temporal modeling and visualization”). When data is collected across time as well as
space and has at least one property (spatial or temporal), we can define a spatial-temporal model. Most natural
language processing formalisms focus on how time and space are expressed in text do make use of some common
features, such as time and space measures in different units or directly expressed temporal and spatial
correlations, out of which partial ordering of events in time and spatial arrangements of objects can be inferred
or predicted. Other very recent techniques get rid of specific features at all and, working directly on the raw
text passed through different layers of a neural network, are able to evidence predictions about time and space, in
a way which is very close to how humans behave when reading. In this survey we review a number of technologies that
deal with time and space in texts, with the intention to lay the basis of further research that should focus on the
temporal and spatial interpretation of the natural language. In particular, we are interested in offering an
implementation of the Time Yards Model (Cristea, 2017; Cristea and Macovei, 2017), with possible theoretical
extensions that make this model applicable to hazards and mass events.

===PAGE 2===
Classical NLP models consider time from a logical perspective (Allen, 1983; Allen & Ferguson, 1994), rather distant
from the way the machine could decipher it in a text. In order to be processed, the text should be regarded as a
set of chronologically and logically connected events, starting from a sequence of textual elements that are linked
through relations. The relations can hold between words, between sentences or between larger spans of text, like
for instance paragraphs, but at the lowest layer they relate textual mentions of events. Some models describing
this behavior will be presented below. In the future we will focus on textual data acquired from a wide variety of
sources, but in this study we consider as a use case a social media channel, the tweets, and a particular type of
event, the Coronavirus pandemic. The interpretation of this type of data can play a vital role in identifying and
preventing hazards, such as pandemics or natural disasters, but we also believe it could be a good source to
inspire us on the most adequate methods to be integrated in our study dedicated to TYM. Time and space textual
patterns, rather frequently used in the semantic interpretation of texts, may be highly complex, so it is a
challenging and demanding process to properly develop them to be suited in various types of event mentioning data.
For instance, in order to analyze the traffic of the virus as mentioned on tweets, that would reveal its impressive
negative effects on the population, such patterns should be able to put in evidence spatial-temporal placement of
the events, in geographical regions and on a timeline, and output a semantic annotation that could be taken as an
interpretation of what happened. We propose a review of the best models and techniques of time and space processing
in view of finding the best models and technologies suitable to be used for the implementation of a theory that
tries to copy on the machine the human ability to unthread time and decipher spatial relations, in social media,
written press or even on creative texts, characters' trajectories or happenings involving a mass of people. Thus,
this investigation will draw the basic knowledge level of the research intended to uncover in texts the occurrence
of events, their involved characters, and their relationship with time and space. Furthermore, to understand better
what an event is, we have to describe a spatial- temporal dataset, rich in spatial (certain places) and temporal
(certain dates and times) data. An example could be the evolution of the COVID-19 mortality rate in China between
December 2019 and March 2020, where the spatial space are located in China and the intended result is a summary of
the COVID-19 mortality evolution in this time interval. In recent years, more and more spatial- temporal models in
social networks have been introduced. A brief summary following [2] is: (1) link- based - models that apply link
analysis (e.g. Page Rank) to recognize experienced/skillful users and locations that have certain special
properties [3, 4]; (2) content-based - models that use data from a user’s profile and the locations’ features [5,
6]; (3) collaborative filtering - models that affect users’ preferences (e.g. location history) [7, 8]; (4) time-
progressive - models that take into account the imminence of an impact (i.e., the immediate, near, and far future)
[9]. The ultimate research question that this report intends to answer is: How to efficiently implement a system
that exploits the spatial and temporal data paired with events on Social Media to recognize critical issues in
real-tim e?

significant information from them. A. D UCKLING Duckling is a programming language, an engine and a tooling system
for expressing, testing, and evaluating composable language rules on textual input strings. It is known to offer
very good results in terms of accuracy and extensibility (Duckling;). Developed by Facebook, Duckling parses texts

indeed, the Duckling engine provides support for all languages, by allowing the creation of customised language
specific rules and the use of specific gazetteers. Thus, the application developer can easily integrate the engine
and localize it to a given language by updating some specific files. Time graining is accomplished by creating

Conclusions This study contains a quick review over some of the most relevant NLP techniques that can be made part
of a system aiming to analyse time and space in free texts, in social media and in other types of texts. We took
into consideration a rather large spectrum of methods, from POS-tagging to sentiment analysis, mostly for two
reasons: because most of them are compulsory modules in our future implementation and even if they are not so (as
could be the case with a sentiment analysis module), the approach used there could be revised to match our needs.
As such, we now have the most significant ingredients, which, we think, could be used to develop a system able to
put in evidence time segments and their reordering based on different classification criteria. As such, time
tracks, revealing destinies of character in free texts, as intended in the TYM, are just one example, but a
diversity of other types of applications could also be envisioned. What is not included in our study is a review of
methods that take into consideration the characters involved in different events (for instance, as actants or
recipients of actions). This represents a compulsory component of the endeavour we envision. Indeed, to refer back
to TYM, a time segment is characterised by three conditions: the unity of time (meaning that all events mentioned
in the text span of the time segment should be either synchronous or linearly ordered on the time axis), the unity
of characters (meaning that the involved characters mentioned are not changing over the text span) and the unity of
perspective (meaning that the events included in a time segment are narrated by a unique teller, being she/he the
